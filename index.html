§ 01
Viscosity

Honey remembers. Water forgets.

Viscosity is a fluid's internal friction: how quickly a push gets shared with the neighbours and lost as heat. Thick fluids smother motion before it can go anywhere. Thin ones let it run, and run, and break apart into eddies. Drag the slider and the water under this text changes its mind.

§ 02
Vorticity

Every eddy is the memory of a push.

Spin is hard to kill. A swirl in a basin outlives the hand that made it, folding dye into thinner and thinner filaments until the eye gives up following. The solver here hands a little of the lost spin back every frame — vorticity confinement — which is why the curls stay sharp instead of going to mush.

§ 03
Surface tension

The skin on the water.

Where a liquid meets air, its molecules pull inward and the surface behaves like a stretched membrane. It is why droplets round themselves off, why two of them reach for each other and merge, and why a pond skater can stand on a pond.

§ 04
Diffusion

Ink is a slow argument with water.

Drop pigment into a still glass and it barely spreads; molecular diffusion is glacially slow. Stir once and it is everywhere in seconds. Mixing is almost entirely the work of motion, not of time. Pick a pigment and stir.
